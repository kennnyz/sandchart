# ADR 0008: Replay across timeframes

Date: 2026-11-28. Status: accepted. Supersedes decision 1 of [ADR 0005](0005-replay.md) and its bar-index command model.

## Context

Replay in 0.4 steps the chart's own timeframe and its trade commands point at bar indices of that timeframe (ADR 0005). Switching the chart from 1h to 15m during a replay therefore has to end it: the 15m bars have other indices, and the forming 1h bar cannot be shown from 1h bars alone. Traders expect what other chart tools do: replay at a chosen step (an "update interval" such as 1m, 15m or 1h), look at any timeframe while doing it, see the forming 4h bar grow as they step, and keep their trades when they switch. The priorities stay reliability first, then speed and low resource use, then minimalism.

## Decision

1. **A replay has a base timeframe, the step resolution.** Stepping reveals base candles. The point the replay has reached, the **cut**, is a time: the close time of the last revealed base candle. A candle's close time is its open time plus the timeframe's length, or the start of the next calendar month for 1M. The base candles revealed at a cut are those that close at or before it.
2. **Chart candles are aggregated in core.** For a chart timeframe T at or above the base, `aggregate` builds T's candles from the revealed base candles: the open of the first, the highest high, the lowest low, the close of the last, the summed volume. Buckets match Binance: fixed-length buckets aligned to the Unix epoch for minutes, hours and days; weeks start on Monday 00:00 UTC; months are calendar months in UTC. 3d is never built from a lower timeframe: no recorded Binance 3d candles confirm where its buckets start, and a guess would show wrong bars (a 3d replay steps native 3d candles). The last bucket may be partial, which is the forming bar. A bucket without base candles (a gap) gives no candle. T is accepted only when every T boundary is a base boundary: 1h from 15m, 1w from 1d and 1M from 1d are fine, 1M from 1w, 1w from 3d and 3d from 1d are refused. T equal to the base returns the base candles unchanged. A chart below the base re-bases the session to that timeframe; that is the engine's job and comes in the next step.
3. **Commands carry a time, not a bar index:** the cut at the moment they were given. An open fills at the close of the base candle ending at that cut; stop and take are checked on the base candles after it; an edit applies from the next base candle; a manual close fills at the close of the base candle ending at its cut. The ambiguity rule (a candle touching both levels is a stop) and the gap rule (a candle opening beyond a level fills at its open) are unchanged and apply to base candles. A trade's entry and exit times are the open times of the base candles it filled on, so a chart of any timeframe puts them in the bucket that contains them. Results therefore do not depend on the chart's timeframe: they are a function of the base candles and the commands only.
4. **The `.replay` file goes to version 2:** the instrument, the base timeframe (`base`), the base candles (context, the revealed ones and optionally later ones), the start (the cut when the replay began), the current cut (`cut`), the commands with their times, and, for a session that caught up with real time, `caughtUpAt` (decision 9): equal to the cut, with no candle after it; a file that breaks either is refused. Version 1 files still open: each bar index becomes that bar's close time, with the version 1 timeframe as the base, and they give the same trades. Saving always writes version 2. The limits and the `sha256` rule (it covers the candles only) are unchanged.
5. **Determinism and limits are unchanged.** Results are computed in Rust only (the browser mock keeps a TEST-ONLY copy checked against the same fixture); a session holds at most 200 000 base candles; future candles are never sent to the UI.
6. **The engine session holds closed base candles only.** It keeps the base, the base candles (context, revealed, later), the start and the cut as times. A candle that has not closed at the engine's clock is never loaded (the cache may hold the newest one as it was while forming), so a start cannot be a forming bar.
7. **A chart of timeframe T in a replay** (`replay_state(tf, …)`) must build from the base: its bars are aggregated from the revealed base candles, the last may be partial (`partial`). Indicators and position outcomes of the replayed pair use the same aggregated bars. A T that does not build from the base is an error that names the fix (re-base to T), never a fallback to the cache, which holds bars after the cut.
8. **Re-basing (`replay_set_base`) keeps the cut, the start and the commands, and plays the commands again on the new base candles.** Results can change with the base: a 1h candle that touched both levels counts as a stop, while its 15m candles may show the take first. A re-base the commands do not fit (a command time with no new candle closing then) is refused and the session stays as it was. A session that caught up (decision 9) stays finished: a lower base loads candles only up to its cut.
   - **Lower base** (the old base builds from it): its candles are loaded from the cache when it holds the whole window, else fetched from the provider page by page into memory only. They are never stored: a window in the middle of the history would break the cached series being contiguous. The window starts before the start with the old context's time span, at most 5 000 candles, and ends now (at the last candle for a file). The part from the start on must fit 200 000 candles, else it is refused with the longest span the step allows (138 days at 1m). A missing candle (an exchange gap) moves the start up and the cut down to candles that exist.
   - **Higher base** (it builds from the old one): its candles are aggregated from the current ones, leaving out a bucket they cover only in part at either end. The cut must be on its boundary, else the re-base is refused with the time to step to: snapping down would hide candles already seen, snapping up would show unseen ones. The start moves up to the next boundary; a command before it makes the re-base fail as above.
   - Any other pair (1w and 1M, 3d and 1d) is refused.
9. **Newer data and catching up.** When a step reveals the last loaded candle of a session started from the cache, the engine loads the newer closed candles: from the cache when they follow without a gap (the chart's live stream stores them), else from the provider, in memory. A failure is reported and the next step tries again; so is an empty answer while a newer candle should have closed (a gap in the exchange's data or a bad answer). When no newer candle can have closed yet (the one after the last closes after the engine's clock), the session has **caught up with real time, and the backtest ends there** (owner decision, 2026-11-28; it replaces the first version of this decision, where the session followed the live candles and open trades kept resolving on them):
   - the cut at that moment is recorded as `caughtUpAt` and never moves again; simulation and statistics use the candles up to it only, so the results are as deterministic as any other session's;
   - the trade still open there is **unfinished** (exit reason `unfinished`, from `Simulation::at_real_time` in core): it is listed with its R marked to the last close, and like an open trade it does not count in the statistics;
   - steps and commands are refused, all of them: the backtest is finished, and the live chart shows prices after the cut;
   - the pair is no longer held back: the chart's live events and alerts flow again, for display only, and indicators and outcomes read the cache. The session opens no live stream of its own;
   - the file saved holds the candles up to `caughtUpAt` and that time, never a live candle, and opens to the same unfinished trade (finished, without live data);
   - a re-base keeps the session finished (decision 8).
   A file's session and a pair without a provider (CSV) never load newer candles: they end at their last one, without catching up.
10. **3d is never built from a lower timeframe.** No recorded Binance 3d candles are in the repository to confirm where its buckets start; with reliability first, 3d is only stepped natively and cannot be a re-base target from below. Enable it with a recorded fixture.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep stepping the chart's timeframe and translate indices on a switch.** No new maths, but the forming higher-timeframe bar cannot be shown, a lower timeframe cannot be shown at all, and every switch rewrites the commands.
- **Always step 1-minute candles.** One resolution for everything, but a year of 1m is 525 600 candles, over the cap and slow to fetch, for traders who step 4h bars.
- **Use the exchange's native higher-timeframe candles next to the base ones.** No aggregation, but they show the whole bar, future included, until the cut reaches its close.
- **Commands as base-candle indices.** Smaller numbers, but indices move when context is prepended and differ between bases; a time means the same on every timeframe.

## Consequences

- A higher-timeframe chart in a replay is built from base candles, so it can differ slightly from the exchange's own candles of that timeframe (as noted in ADR 0005); outside replay the chart keeps the native ones.
- Version 1 files open in this release; older releases refuse version 2 files, as with every format bump.
- The engine session and its IPC (`replay_start(base)`, `replay_step` returning the cut, `replay_set_base`, `replay_state(tf, cursor, tradesFrom)` with `base`, `caughtUp`, `caughtUpAt` and `partial`) work on the base and the cut. The UI starts a replay at the chart's timeframe; switching the chart to a timeframe that builds from the base shows it, a lower one re-bases to it, and the update interval picks the base.
- A caught-up replay costs no extra live stream: the chart's own stream shows the live data.
- A trade still open at real time has no result. Traders who want one close it before catching up.
